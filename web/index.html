            What a passing result means
            
      
            
        Every content_hash and chain_hash was recomputed from the
        record itself (RFC 8785 canonical JSON, SHA-256) and matched, every record links to
        the declared chain_hash of its predecessor, and sequence numbers increase
        by one per stream. A change to a hashed member of a record, or a record reordered or
        removed from between two others, would have shown up, unless every record after it was
        sealed again.
      
            
      
            
        It does not say that the payloads are true, that the clocks were right, that nobody
        sealed the chain again from some record onward, or that the file is complete: records
        cut off at its end leave no trace. Members that are not hashed (job_id, correlation_id,
        decision_id, context, redaction, anchor,
        signature) can be changed without a finding; redaction claims
        which payload members were masked, and nothing protects that claim. Signatures bind the
        chain to a key: give the public key above to have them checked, and tick
        Require a signature on every record, because without it a journal whose
        signatures were removed still passes. Anchoring the latest chain_hash
        elsewhere binds it to a point in time and shows whether records are missing at the end.
        A file without any record never passes. The specification lists these limits in
        section 6.6.
      
            
      
            
        Every record gets one of four states. valid: all checks ran and passed.
        invalid: a check failed (schema_invalid, hash_mismatch,
        chain_break, seq_mismatch, signature_invalid,
        profile_invalid). unknown: the record's own hashes are correct, but its
        predecessor is not in the file, so its link cannot be checked (seq_gap); a record that
        links correctly to it is valid relative to it (after_unknown).
        outside scope: the record names another format or major version and is not checked.
      
            
      
            
        The journal gets one of three verdicts. valid: every record is valid.
        invalid: at least one record is invalid. incomplete: no record is
        invalid, but at least one is unknown or outside the scope, so the file cannot show that nothing is
        missing; deleting a record from the middle leads here. Incomplete fails unless you allow it, which is
        meant for journals known to be incomplete, such as rotated logs.
      
            
      
            
        A record's profile state (none, valid, invalid,
        outside_scope) says whether its payload was checked against the schema of its profile. A
        profile this page does not know leaves the payload unchecked but does not change the verdict, unless
        Require known profiles is ticked.
